# No Surprises Act — Quick-Rights Reference Sheet

*Companion to Module 7. A one-page plain-English reference to federal surprise-billing protections. Keep it handy when a bill looks like a surprise.*

> **Disclaimer:** Educational summary only — not legal advice. Protections have conditions and exceptions, and state laws may add more. Verify how the rules apply to your specific bill, and ask your insurer/regulator if unsure.

---

## What "surprise billing" protection generally covers

In common situations, you are protected from **out-of-network "balance bills"** — and you typically owe only your **in-network cost-sharing** (the deductible/copay/coinsurance you'd pay in-network). The rest is settled between the provider and insurer, not billed to you:

- ✅ **Emergency care** — including from out-of-network hospitals/providers, and (in many cases) post-stabilization care.
- ✅ **Non-emergency care at an in-network facility** delivered by an **out-of-network clinician you didn't choose** — e.g., anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, assistant surgeons, hospitalists.
- ✅ **Air ambulance** (out-of-network) — protected in many cases. (Ground ambulance is often **not** covered by these federal protections — check your state.)

## What you typically owe in protected situations

- Only your **in-network deductible, copay, and coinsurance** for the service.
- **Not** the difference between the provider's charge and what insurance paid.

## Good-faith estimate (for uninsured / self-pay patients)

- If you're **uninsured or paying yourself**, you're generally entitled to a **good-faith estimate** of expected charges before scheduled care.
- If your **final bill is substantially higher** than the estimate (commonly by $400 or more over the estimate), you may be able to use a **patient-provider dispute resolution** process. Verify the current threshold and process.

## Red flags that you may have a surprise bill

- 🚩 An ER visit where a separate provider billed you out-of-network
- 🚩 A bill from an anesthesiologist/radiologist/pathologist you never chose
- 🚩 A balance bill for the gap between "billed" and "paid" after in-network facility care
- 🚩 A bill far above a good-faith estimate you were given

## What to say / do

1. **State it plainly to the biller and insurer:** "This appears to be a surprise/balance bill protected under the federal No Surprises Act. Please reprocess so I owe only my in-network cost-sharing."
2. **Put it in writing**, attach the bill and EOB, and request a corrected statement.
3. **Don't pay the disputed surprise amount** while it's being corrected (pay only your in-network cost-share if you can).
4. **Escalate** to your insurer's appeals process (Module 5) and to regulators if it isn't fixed.

## Where to get help

- The **federal No Surprises Help Desk** (search "No Surprises Act help desk") handles complaints.
- Your **state Department of Insurance** — especially for ground ambulance and state-specific protections.
- Keep records of every call and letter (see your case file).

> Bottom line: if you got care you didn't choose to be out-of-network, or an emergency bill, **don't assume the balance is yours.** Assert the protection, in writing, and make them prove otherwise.
