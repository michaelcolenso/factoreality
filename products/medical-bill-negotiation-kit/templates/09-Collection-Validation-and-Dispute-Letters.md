# Collection Agency Validation / Dispute Letter Templates

*Companion to Module 7. When a third-party collector contacts you, these letters are your first and most powerful moves. Send in writing — keep copies and proof of mailing (certified mail with return receipt is ideal).*

> **Disclaimer:** Educational templates only — not legal advice. The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) governs third-party collectors and gives you specific rights and deadlines. Act promptly — you generally have a limited window (commonly 30 days from the collector's first notice) to dispute and request validation. Verify the timeline on your notice.

---

## First moves when a collector contacts you

1. **Don't admit the debt, don't pay, don't panic** on first contact.
2. **Send a validation request in writing, promptly** (within the dispute window). This **pauses collection** until they validate.
3. **Make them prove** the amount, the original creditor, and their right to collect.

---

## Letter 1 — Debt Validation Request

```
[Your Name]
[Address] | [Date]

[Collection Agency Name]
[Address]

RE: Demand for Validation of Debt
Account / Reference #: [#]
Alleged original creditor: [if known]

To Whom It May Concern:

I am responding to your contact about the debt referenced above. I dispute
this debt and request that you validate it. Under my rights, please cease
collection activity until you provide the following in writing:

1. The amount of the alleged debt and an itemized breakdown.
2. The name and address of the original creditor.
3. Documentation showing I am the responsible party for this debt.
4. Proof that you are licensed/authorized to collect this debt in my state
   and that it is within any applicable time limits.

Until you provide this validation, please do not continue collection
efforts and do not report this debt to the credit bureaus. Please direct
all communication about this matter to me in writing at the address above.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Printed Name]
```

## Letter 2 — Dispute (when the debt is wrong)

```
[Your Name]
[Address] | [Date]

[Collection Agency Name]
[Address]

RE: Dispute of Debt — Account / Reference #: [#]

To Whom It May Concern:

I dispute the debt referenced above for the following reason(s):

[Check/describe all that apply:
 - This debt was already PAID. Proof of payment enclosed.
 - My INSURANCE should have paid this; the claim was mishandled and is
   being corrected/appealed with the provider and insurer.
 - A FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE / charity-care application was pending or I was
   never screened for assistance before collection.
 - The AMOUNT is incorrect. (Correct amount/itemization needed.)
 - This is NOT MY DEBT / mistaken identity.
 - The bill contained BILLING ERRORS that were never corrected.
 - This appears to be a SURPRISE/BALANCE BILL I am protected from.]

Documentation supporting my dispute is enclosed: [list].

Please cease collection activity, do not report this to the credit bureaus
while disputed (or correct/delete any existing report), and confirm in
writing how you will resolve this. If this account was placed in error,
please recall it to the original creditor.

Sincerely,
[Signature] [Printed Name]
```

---

## Letter 3 — Credit-Bureau Dispute (inaccurate collection on your report)

```
[Your Name] — [Address] — [Date]
[Credit Bureau Name — dispute address]

RE: Dispute of Inaccurate Item
Report/Confirmation #: [if any]
Disputed item: [Collector name, account #, amount, date reported]

To Whom It May Concern:

The above item on my credit report is inaccurate and I request its
correction or deletion. [State why: paid; wrong amount; insurance should
have paid; duplicate; not mine; reported in violation of current rules;
previously disputed/resolved.] Documentation is enclosed.

Please investigate this item with the furnisher and correct or remove it.
Please send me the results of your investigation.

Sincerely,
[Signature] [Printed Name]
```

---

## Optional — "Cease Contact" Request

> Note: You can ask a collector in writing to stop contacting you. This does **not** erase the debt, and it can change your strategic position — consider getting legal advice before using it. A validation/dispute letter is usually the better first move.

```
Please cease all contact with me regarding account [#], except to confirm
that contact will stop or to notify me of a specific legal action.
```

## After you send

- [ ] Keep a copy of every letter + the certified-mail receipt
- [ ] Log the date sent and the response deadline
- [ ] If they keep collecting/reporting without validating → escalate (federal consumer-finance regulator, state AG) and consider a consumer-rights attorney
- [ ] If insurance/aid is the real issue → loop back to Modules 2, 4, and 5
