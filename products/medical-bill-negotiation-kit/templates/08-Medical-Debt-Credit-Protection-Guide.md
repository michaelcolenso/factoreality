# Medical Debt & Credit Protection Quick Guide

*Companion to Module 7. How to keep medical debt from wrecking your credit — and clean it up if it already has.*

> **Disclaimer:** Educational guide only — not legal or financial advice. Credit-reporting rules for medical debt have been changing and may differ by the time you read this. **Verify current rules** and check your actual credit reports before relying on any specific threshold or timeline.

---

## 1. Pull and read your credit reports

- [ ] Get your reports from **all three** major bureaus (you're entitled to free copies — search the official free annual report site).
- [ ] Look specifically for **medical collection accounts**.
- [ ] For each, note: the collector, the amount, the original creditor, and the date reported.

## 2. Know the (evolving) landscape

Recent years have brought consumer-friendly changes to how medical debt appears on credit reports — for example, voluntary bureau changes around **paid** medical collections, a **waiting period** before medical collections can appear, **dollar thresholds** below which they aren't reported, and regulatory efforts to **limit medical debt on credit reports** more broadly.

> These specifics change. **Verify the current rules** and, more importantly, check what's actually on *your* reports — then fix inaccuracies.

## 3. Hunt for errors (they're common)

Flag any entry that is:

- [ ] **Already paid** but still showing a balance
- [ ] **An amount you don't recognize** or that's wrong
- [ ] **A debt insurance should have paid** (sent back to provider/insurer)
- [ ] **Under a financial-assistance application** that was pending or never offered
- [ ] **A duplicate** of another entry
- [ ] **Not your debt** (mistaken identity)
- [ ] **Too old / reported too soon** under current rules
- [ ] **Already disputed/resolved** but reappeared

## 4. Dispute inaccuracies — two fronts

1. **With the credit bureaus:** file a dispute (online, by mail, or phone) for each inaccurate entry, with documentation. The bureau must investigate, typically within about 30 days, and correct or remove unverifiable items.
2. **With the collector/furnisher:** dispute directly and demand they correct what they report (see the **Collection Agency Validation / Dispute Letter Templates**).

Keep copies of every dispute and every response, and track the deadlines.

## 5. Prevent future damage

- **Don't let pending balances slide into collections** — get holds in writing during disputes, appeals, and aid applications (Modules 2, 4, 5).
- **Settle with "no negative reporting"** language in writing (Module 3 / Part A of the settlement framework).
- **Pay plans on time** once agreed, and keep the written terms.

## 6. When to escalate

- **The bureau/collector ignores a valid dispute** → escalate; file a complaint with the federal consumer-finance regulator and your **state Attorney General**.
- **A collector violated the law** (kept collecting after a valid dispute, reported known-false info) → a **consumer-rights attorney** may help, sometimes at no upfront cost.

## Quick action checklist

- [ ] All three reports pulled
- [ ] Medical collections listed and reviewed
- [ ] Inaccurate entries disputed with bureaus **and** collectors (with proof)
- [ ] Pending/appealed/aid balances protected from collections
- [ ] Settlements documented with "no negative reporting"
- [ ] Complaints/attorney considered for violations
