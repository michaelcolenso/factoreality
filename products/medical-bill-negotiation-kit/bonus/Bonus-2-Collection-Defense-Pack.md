# BONUS — Collection Defense Pack

*Upsell bonus module. For when medical debt has already gone to one or more collection agencies, or a collector is being aggressive.*

> **Disclaimer:** Educational resource only — not legal advice. The FDCPA and related laws give you rights against third-party collectors; specifics and time limits vary by state and change over time. For lawsuits, garnishment threats, or rights violations, consult a licensed consumer-rights attorney.

---

## 1. Your rights at a glance (third-party collectors)

Under federal law, debt collectors generally **may not**:

- Call at unreasonable hours or harass, threaten, or use abusive language
- Lie about the amount owed or pretend to be law enforcement/attorneys
- Threaten actions they can't or won't take
- Continue collecting after you've requested validation (until they validate)
- Contact you at work after you tell them to stop, or contact you after a valid written cease request (with limited exceptions)
- Discuss your debt with third parties (beyond limited contact to locate you)

If a collector does these things, **document it** (dates, times, what was said) — violations can be leverage and may give rise to a claim.

## 2. The defense sequence

1. **Validate first.** Send the **Debt Validation Request** (in the Collection Letters template) within the dispute window. Collection must pause until they validate.
2. **Dispute if wrong.** Use the **Dispute** letter with proof (paid, insurance, aid pending, errors, not mine, surprise bill).
3. **Send it back to the source.** Many medical "debts" in collections trace to billing errors, wrong insurance, or skipped financial-assistance screening — push to have the account **recalled to the original provider**.
4. **Fix the credit report.** Dispute inaccurate entries with the bureaus and the collector (Module 7 + Credit guide).
5. **Escalate violations.** File complaints with the federal consumer-finance regulator and your **state Attorney General**; consult an attorney for FDCPA violations.

## 4. Special situations

- **You're being sued:** **Do not ignore a lawsuit.** Missing a court date can lead to a default judgment (and possible wage garnishment). Respond by the deadline and **get a lawyer** — many consumer-rights attorneys offer free consultations, and you may have strong defenses (no validation, wrong amount, insurance/aid issues, time-barred debt).
- **Time-barred debt:** Old debts may be past the legal time limit to sue ("statute of limitations") — but **be careful: paying or even acknowledging an old debt can restart the clock** in some places. Get advice before acting on very old debts.
- **Threats of garnishment/liens:** Take these seriously and seek legal help; protections and procedures vary by state.
- **Aggressive/abusive contact:** Log everything; send a written dispute (and, if appropriate, a cease-contact request); report violations.

## 5. Settling a debt that's already in collections

If a debt is valid and you want to resolve it:

- Negotiate a **reduced lump-sum settlement** (collectors often accept a fraction; use the Settlement Framework).
- **Get "pay for delete" / no-negative-reporting in writing** *before* paying, where possible.
- **Never pay until written terms arrive**, and keep proof of payment forever.
- Confirm the account will be reported as **paid/settled** (or deleted) and **closed**.

## 6. Help resources

- **Federal consumer-finance regulator** — complaints about collectors and credit reporting
- **State Attorney General / consumer-protection office**
- **Nonprofit credit counseling** (certified, low/no-cost)
- **Consumer-rights / FDCPA attorneys** — often free consult; may recover fees from violators
- **Legal aid** — for income-qualifying individuals

## Defense checklist

- [ ] Every account validated (or validation demanded)
- [ ] Disputes sent with proof where the debt is wrong
- [ ] Error/insurance/aid accounts pushed back to the provider
- [ ] Credit-report entries disputed
- [ ] Lawsuit deadlines (if any) calendared and attorney engaged
- [ ] Violations documented and reported
- [ ] Any settlement documented in writing before payment
